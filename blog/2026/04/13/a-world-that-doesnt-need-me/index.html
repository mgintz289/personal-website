April 13, 2026: A world that doesn't need me

It was pointed out to me recently that mathematicians seem to be taking the AI boom harder than many. [This might be because mathematicians want to be heroes CITE noahpinion]. I think this is a pretty good explanation. Even though my departure from mathematics is (nominally) not AI-motivated, and I (unlike some of my peers) am not particularly heartbroken about leaving my mathematical skills behind, I still find myself struggling to find a place where I can make a difference, probably using this meterstick. Especially [since I don't really want to be a tokenmaxxer]. It's felt like everywhere I turn, when I think of a way to make a difference, it's something that AI could do easily, so what's the point? I think a lot of this is the feeling that what I bring to the table is intelligence. And intelligence is very close to the thing that AI can do. But it feels like, then, the problem is universal: that there's nothing that I can do to make the world a better place, because AI can do it just as well. I'm left with two outstanding attacks to this: 1. Is using AI to solve the problem I have in mind worth the environmental cost? If no, I can't say I'm not making a difference (but my difference in the world will be environmental). 2. If there's nothing I can contribute, this means that all of the problems out there are not problems I think I can solve either without AI or with AI in a way thats environmentally sufficient.
